# Hackathon Lessons: From Participant to Winner

After participating in over 4 hackathons and taking home multiple prizes (including 1st place at Hack4Health 2025! 🏆), I've learned some valuable lessons I wish I knew from the start.

## The Winning Formula

### 1. Start With the Problem, Not the Tech

It's tempting to think "I want to use this cool AI model" and build something around it. **Don't.**

The projects that win are the ones that solve real problems. At Hack4Health, we didn't start with "let's use computer vision." We started with "medical students need better ways to learn X-ray analysis."

### 2. Scope Ruthlessly

You have 24-48 hours. You cannot build everything. Here's my framework:

| Priority | What to Build |
|----------|---------------|
| Must Have | Core functionality that solves the problem |
| Should Have | Polish features that make it demo-ready |
| Nice to Have | Everything else (probably won't happen) |

### 3. The Demo is Everything

Judges spend maybe 3 minutes with your project. Make every second count:

- **Hook them in 10 seconds**: What's the problem? Why should they care?
- **Show, don't tell**: Live demos beat slides every time
- **Have a backup**: Record a video in case live demo fails

## Team Dynamics

The best hackathon teams I've been on had:

1. **Clear roles**: Designer, frontend, backend, presenter
2. **Constant communication**: Quick standups every few hours
3. **No ego**: Be willing to cut features or change direction

## The Real Value

Winning is great, but the real value of hackathons is:

- **Networking**: I've met co-founders, mentors, and friends
- **Learning**: Building under pressure teaches you fast
- **Portfolio**: Every project is a talking point in interviews

## Go Build Something

If you haven't done a hackathon yet, find one and sign up. You'll learn more in 48 hours than in weeks of tutorials.
